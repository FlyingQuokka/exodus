## Strategist's Note

**Types that dominated.** The set clusters hard around three patterns: **Identity/Status Attack** (6 hooks) and **Visceral Metaphor/Reframe** (4 hooks) are the center of gravity, with a tight, lethal **Impossible Question** group (3) doing the sharpest work per word. Everything else — confession, warning, exposé, contrast — appears as one-to-three strong singletons. No hook landed below L3; the brief's emotion ("the list was never yours") is so load-bearing that even the weakest entries detonate on contact. No who-cares flags.

**Primed vs. raw.** Both pools are excellent, but they have distinct personalities. **Primed (P)** leans *prosecutorial and sacred-belief-driven* — it weaponizes the reader's own identity ("you broke your own first commandment," "you'd call that a scam"), and its back-half hooks (6, 8, 9, 10) carry the full product payload and CTA, making them closer to ready-to-ship ad bodies. **Raw (R)** leans *novel-image and shock-metaphor* — it invented the three freshest angles in the entire set: the landlord changing the locks, the 1850 sharecropper, and the "lobotomized" surviving bot. Raw is more *surprising*; primed is more *surgical and conversion-complete*. Raw also produced the only confession-style hook (the "400 people" open), which primed never attempted.

**L3 count.** 22 of 22 hit L3. That's a function of an unusually strong brief rather than inflated grading — every hook sets stakes and attacks identity in the opening lines.

**The 3 I'd test first:**
1. **[R] "Name one customer who bought your GPT."** — The purest stop-scroll device in the set. Seven-word opener, impossible for the target to answer, zero wind-up.
2. **[P] "The list is the business. Every imer alive knows this."** — Converts the audience's gospel into the murder weapon. The most *on-brand* psychological hit for this exact niche.
3. **[R] "Your GPT 'survived' the last migration. But its actions were stripped..."** — The most novel angle; names a pain they felt but couldn't articulate. Test as the *differentiation* play against the two identity attacks.

Pair recommendation: run #1 and #2 as the shame/identity cohort, #3 as the curiosity/novelty challenger.
