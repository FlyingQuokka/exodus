## Audience Concerns

- Will this platform pull the rug the way OpenAI did? This is the most live concern given the market's fresh wound. Answer with a choice of four models rather than one vendor, published fees rather than an undisclosed formula and a data export, so you are never tied to one platform and can take your bots elsewhere if you ever want to.
- What does the platform take from my sales? Answer plainly: a published fee on every sale, 5% of the price after tax on Apprentice to Expert or 3% on Pro and Master, plus 30 cents.
- Can I really make money selling bots? Answer with three clear ways to sell: access through a paywall, resale rights through Multi-sell and outright sale through Transfer. Be upfront that Bot Market selling starts at Pro.
- Is this just another bot builder? Answer with the rights-based Bot Market in language imers already use (RR, MRR and PLR style) and with affiliate shares that sellers set on each listing.
- Can I trust a new platform with real income? Answer with plain terms: published fees, cancellation that keeps access to the end of the period, a refund of the unused remainder on annual plans and a Founders offer that locks the discount in for life.
- Can a non-developer build and embed a bot? Answer with the no-code builder, the embeddable widget and the importer for moving an old GPT across.
- Can I see who uses my bot? Answer with analytics from the Novice plan, your own bot page and the option to require sign-up. Never promise a customer list or email addresses.

## Brand Voice

Speaks the imer's own language: PLR, MRR, RR, funnels, OTOs, affiliates, boosts, tiers, not generic SaaS terms. Direct and plain, confident without corporate hedging, aimed at people who already sell online. Speaks as the product, never as a named founder. Calls the member area the Botter's Lab. Names the OpenAI shutdown directly without gloating, since this audience lived through it. Leans on proof over promises: published fees, a live embedded demo and exact numbers (50% of a referred member's first payment, 20% of every payment after) rather than vague reward language. Never promises income. Writes the name as Botterverse in prose and Botterverse only as the domain.

## Awareness Primers

### Unaware / Problem-Aware

Rules: ground every hook only in confirmed facts: four models chosen per bot; three ways to sell; a published platform fee of 5% or 3% plus 30 cents; Bot Market selling from Pro; the embeddable widget; the importer; the affiliate programme paying 50% of a referred member's first payment and 20% of every payment after; a Founders offer of 50% off for life with a permanent badge. Address the live wound, OpenAI closing new GPT creation to personal accounts in August 2026, without gloating. Use the market's real complaints: no published revenue share, no ownership, no way to contact your users, poor discovery and sudden removal with very little notice. Never mention the Empire tier. Never name a competitor. Never name or quote a founder. Never invent a testimonial or a stat. Never promise income.

Example hooks:

1. OpenAI closed new GPT creation to personal accounts with very little notice. Ask who actually owns the next bot you build.
2. You built an audience on a bot you never owned. It lived behind someone else's login under someone else's branding.
3. The GPT Store never told you its payout formula. Botterverse publishes its fee: 3% or 5% plus 30 cents.
4. Every imer replacing GPT income is stitching together three or four tools that were never built to work together.
5. There is a difference between a platform that lets you sell a bot and a platform that lets you sell rights to a bot the way you already sell rights to an info product.

### Solution-Aware / Product-Aware

Rules: for botters already comparing alternatives. Lead with what is distinctive: the rights-based Bot Market (Multi-sell and Transfer), the published fee, the choice of four models and affiliate shares on Bot Market listings. Never name a competitor. Never mention the Empire tier. Reference proof only when real proof exists.

Example angles:

1. Most platforms pay you through a formula you cannot see. Here the fee is published on every tier.
2. Multi-sell is the PLR deal you already know applied to a bot: your buyer resells it under your name or their own.
3. Set an affiliate share on each Bot Market listing and let other Botters sell it for you.
4. A live bot answering questions on this very page is the embeddable widget working in front of you.
5. Founders pay 50% less for life and wear the Founders badge permanently.
