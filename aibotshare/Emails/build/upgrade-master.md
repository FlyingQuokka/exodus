## UM02 - Your first A/B test (day 1, Master upgrade)

**Subject:** [Botter's Lab] Your first A/B test

**Body:**

Hi {{contact.first_name}},

Recap: yesterday we covered what Master Botter adds and I said we'd take your first A/B test from a blank page to Start the test.

New topic: today you set the test up. The next email gives you the wording to paste in, but the structure comes first.

**How a test is built**

The bot as it stands is the control. Every test compares one or more variants against it.

Select [A/B tests](https://lab.{{domain}}/bots/ab-tests) under Bots in the menu then choose the Bot. Set the Control's share (%). That's the portion of visitors who keep seeing the bot as it is.

Add a variant. Each variant has a Variant name, a Share (%) and its own Instructions and Starters. Press Add a variant for more. The shares of all versions must total 100%.

Press Save and the test is stored as a Draft. Press Start the test when you're ready. While it's Running, the same visitor always sees the same version, so a person's chat doesn't change halfway through.

A test stops by itself if the bot is sold by Transfer or deleted. It also stops if your plan drops below Master Botter.

**A setup to start from**

For example, with one variant:

> Bot: [YOUR BOT]
> Control's share (%): 50
> Variant name: [WHAT IS DIFFERENT, FOR EXAMPLE WARMER GREETING]
> Share (%): 50

**Task for today**

Create the test for the bot you chose yesterday and press Save so it sits as a Draft. Leave the variant's Instructions and Starters alone for now. They come next.

Speak soon,

Your Botter Support Team

P.S. Next up: what to test first, with five ideas and the wording to paste in.

**Notes**

## UM03 - What to test first (day 3, Master upgrade)

**Subject:** [Botter's Lab] Five things worth testing first

**Body:**

Hi {{contact.first_name}},

Recap: last time you set up the test as a Draft and I said we'd look at what to test first, with five ideas and the wording to paste in.

New topic: change one thing per test. If a variant differs in two ways, you won't know which one caused the result.

**Five tests**

In each case the control is your bot as it stands. The variant's Instructions box replaces your bot's instructions for that version, so copy the control's instructions into it first. Then add the wording below or change only the matching line. Starters go in the variant's Starters.

1. Greeting - which first reply keeps people talking.

> Open every first reply with one short friendly sentence that names the topic of the visitor's question.

2. Tone - warm against formal.

> Answer in a warm and relaxed voice, as if talking to a friend.

3. Answer length - short against full.

> Keep every answer to three sentences or fewer unless the visitor asks for more.

4. Starters - which opening questions bring visitors in. Put these in the variant's Starters.

> What can you help me with?
> Where should I begin?
> [YOUR MOST COMMON QUESTION]

5. Closing line - whether an invitation keeps the chat going.

> End every answer by asking if the visitor would like more detail on any part of it.

**Which first**

Start with Starters or the greeting. They shape the first impression and they show their effect quickly.

**Task for today**

Pick one idea. Paste it into the variant you created. Then check that the shares total 100% and press Start the test.

Speak soon,

Your Botter Support Team

## UM04 - Read the results (day 5, Master upgrade)

**Subject:** [Botter's Lab] Which of the five measures to read first

**Body:**

Hi {{contact.first_name}},

Recap: last time you started a test and I said we'd look at which of the five measures to read first.

New topic: the Results show five figures for each version. Each one answers a different question, so pick one before you read.

**The five measures**

1. Conversations - how many chats each version had. It tells you whether the split is working and whether you have enough data yet.
2. Messages per conversation - how long chats last. A higher figure can mean people are interested. It can also mean the bot isn't answering fully. Each message uses credits, so read it with care.
3. Returning customers - who came back. This is the strongest sign that a version is useful.
4. Paywall conversion - the share of that version's paywall views that led to a successful payment for access. It applies only to bots that sell access and shows No views if there were none.
5. Revenue per conversation - your share of the access payments and message top-ups credited to that version, divided by its conversations. Bot Market sales are not included. It shows No data if there were no conversations.

An info mark beside each figure explains it.

**Choosing the one that matters**

A free bot is judged on Returning customers and Messages per conversation. A paid bot is judged on Paywall conversion and Revenue per conversation.

Decide this before the numbers arrive. It stops you picking the figure that happens to favour your favourite version.

**A reading guide**

For example, write this down for each test:

> Bot: [NAME]
> What I changed: [ONE THING]
> The measure that decides it: [ONE OF THE FIVE]
> I will look again on: [DATE]

**Task for today**

Open the Results of your running test. Write down the one measure that decides it and the date to look again.

Speak soon,

Your Botter Support Team

P.S. Next up: a test has a result, so what do you do with it and which of four buttons ends it?

**Notes**
- Facts: the five Results measures with these exact labels (guide 8.3). Info mark beside every figure (8.1). Each message uses credits (10.1).
- Confirmed by the app agent (answer 9): Paywall conversion is new paywall accesses with a successful payment divided by that version's paywall views. Revenue per conversation is the owner's net share of successful access payments and top-ups divided by conversations. Bot Market sales are in neither.

## UM05 - Crown a winner (day 7, Master upgrade)

**Subject:** [Botter's Lab] What to do with a test result

**Body:**

Hi {{contact.first_name}},

Recap: last time we looked at the five measures. I said we'd see what to do with a result and which of four buttons ends a test.

New topic: when you finish a test, you have four choices.

**The four buttons**

1. Choose as winner - for a version that clearly beat the control. The version's instructions become the bot's new main version and its starters replace yours. Chats already open stay on the version they began on. The name, model, knowledge, price and paywall don't change.
2. Keep the control - for a test where nothing beat it.
3. Stop the test - to end it without choosing.
4. Start a new test - to try the next idea.

**A decision rule**

A small gap is not a result. For example, use this rule:

> 1. Wait until every version has had at least [NUMBER, FOR EXAMPLE 30] conversations.
> 2. Look only at the measure you chose before the test began.
> 3. If a version leads on that measure and isn't behind on Returning customers, choose it as the winner.
> 4. If the gap is small, keep the control and test something bigger.

Write down what you learned in one line. After five tests, that list is the most useful document you own.

> Test: [WHAT I CHANGED]
> Result: [WINNER OR CONTROL]
> Why I think so: [ONE LINE]

**Task for today**

If your test has reached the number you set, finish it with one of the buttons. If not, leave it running and write your decision rule now.

Speak soon,

Your Botter Support Team

## UM06 - Views against purchases (day 9, Master upgrade)

**Subject:** [Botter's Lab] Where visitors drop out

**Body:**

Hi {{contact.first_name}},

Recap: last time we covered how to end a test and I said we'd see where visitors drop out between viewing and buying.

New topic: that's the Conversion panel, part of the advanced analytics on Master Botter. It sets what people viewed against what they bought.

**The panel**

Open [Analytics](https://lab.{{domain}}/bots/analytics) in the menu and choose a bot that sells access. The Conversion panel shows four figures:

1. Paywall views - how many people reached the paywall.
2. Listing views - how many people viewed the bot's Bot Market listing.
3. Purchases - how many bought.
4. Conversion - the share that bought.

Use the Date range control to compare two periods. Each visitor counts once per day for each bot and views from today appear tomorrow. A bot that isn't listed shows 0 Listing views.

**A diagnosis checklist**

For example, find your case and try the fix:

> Many paywall views and few purchases: the paywall isn't convincing. Check the description, the conversation starters shown under Things you can ask it, the Message cap, the Money-back guarantee and Show the bot's rating on its paywall page.
>
> Many listing views and few purchases: the listing isn't convincing. Check the description, tags and price.
>
> Few listing views: not enough people see the listing. Check the category, tags and the Affiliate share (%) which rewards members who promote it.
>
> Few paywall views: not enough people reach the bot. Share the link in more places.

Change one thing then compare the next period.

**Task for today**

Open the Conversion panel for one paid bot. Write down the four figures and decide which case you're in. Make one change.

## UM07 - What you cannot test (day 11, Master upgrade)

**Subject:** [Botter's Lab] The one thing a test never changes

**Body:**

Hi {{contact.first_name}},

Recap: last time we looked at the Conversion panel and I said we'd cover the one thing no test will ever change.

New topic: it's the price. A/B tests compare Instructions and Starters. They never test your price. Anything else on the bot changes one setting at a time outside a test.

**What a test can change**

A variant has its own Instructions and Starters. Those are the two things a test compares.

**What you change yourself**

Change these on the bot itself. Price changes apply to new buyers only and existing buyers keep their access.

1. One-time price.
2. Message cap, top-up size and top-up price.
3. Money-back guarantee (No guarantee, 7 days or 14 days).
4. The paywall description.
5. The model. On a Public bot, a model change means confirming declarations again and a dearer model uses more credits per message.

Change one setting. Note the date. Compare the same Date range before and after in [Analytics](https://lab.{{domain}}/bots/analytics). Don't change the control's own settings while a test runs, because the results stop being comparable.

**A change log**

For example, keep one line per change:

> Date: [DATE]
> Bot: [NAME]
> Changed: [SETTING] from [OLD] to [NEW]
> Look again on: [DATE]

## UM08 - Credits at scale (day 13, Master upgrade)

**Subject:** [Botter's Lab] Stop a busy week emptying your credits

**Body:**

Hi {{contact.first_name}},

Recap: last time we covered what a test cannot change and I said we'd look at how to stop a busy week emptying your credits.

New topic: every message in every version of a test uses your credits and so do your own previews. A bigger test means more messages.

**Three protections**

1. Daily credit limit. Open the bot from [Bot list](https://lab.{{domain}}/bots) then open Sharing and embed. Enter whole credits. Free visitors are told the bot has reached its limit for the day. Paid buyers and you carry on. It resets at midnight UTC and you're emailed once that day.
2. Auto top-up. Open [Billing and credits](https://lab.{{domain}}/billing) then Auto top-up. Read the statement then press Agree and switch on. Set Top up when my credits fall below and the Top-up amount. It needs an active plan and a card on file. It emails you each time it charges.
3. Warnings. You're emailed when credits fall below your top-up threshold and again when credits can't cover a message. At that point, all your bots stop answering everyone until credits are added.

The usage graph on Billing and credits shows Messages, Plan credits and Credits bought over time, so you can see which days run heavy.

**A credit plan**

For example, go through these in order:

> 1. Open the usage graph and note the busiest day of the last month.
> 2. Set the Top up when my credits fall below value to cover at least one busiest day.
> 3. Set a Daily credit limit on any bot that runs a test.
> 4. Check your credit balance in the credits pill at the top of the page before you start a new test.

**Task for today**

Work through the plan above. Switch on Auto top-up if you haven't already.

## UM09 - Tests and the CRM (day 15, Master upgrade)

**Subject:** [Botter's Lab] Which version did each customer meet

**Body:**

Hi {{contact.first_name}},

Recap: last time we covered how to protect your credits and I said your CRM can see which version each customer met.

New topic: every event the CRM connector sends carries the A/B variant. Your own CRM can then compare the customers of each version.

**What is sent**

Each event holds the event name, a tag, the customer's name and email address, the bot's name and slug, the A/B variant, the time and an event id. Only customers who ticked the box to share their details are included. Customers without an account are never sent.

If you haven't connected your CRM yet, open [Settings](https://lab.{{domain}}/settings) then CRM. You need a Webhook address and Your privacy notice. Then press Save and keep the signing secret that appears once. Press Send test event and Switch on. The [help article](https://{{domain}}/docs/crm-connector/) covers each step.

**Segmenting by variant**

Lifecycle tags fill only the bot's slug where you write {bot}. The variant arrives as a field in the event, so segment by that field in your CRM.

For example, in your CRM:

> Segment A: A/B variant equals [CONTROL] and tag equals buyer-{bot}
> Segment B: A/B variant equals [VARIANT NAME] and tag equals buyer-{bot}
> Compare the size of each segment against the Conversations figure in the test Results.

Do the same with engaged-{bot}, ready-to-buy-{bot} or any lifecycle tag you set.

**A caution**

Your CRM holds only the customers who agreed to share their details, so its numbers are a sample. The test Results cover every visitor. Use the Results to decide the winner. Use the CRM to understand who each version attracts.

You are responsible for this data under data protection law and you must honour every unsubscribe.

**Task for today**

Create the two segments in your CRM for your current test. Write the tag you'll compare.
