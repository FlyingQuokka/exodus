# What GPT builders are saying about the withdrawal

Researched 19 October 2026 from public sources. Internal use only: never name competitors or quote people in copy. Short quotes are marked. Everything else is paraphrase.

## Facts that frame the pain

- Personal accounts (Free, Go, Plus and Pro) lost the ability to create or publish new custom GPTs on 16 August 2026. One source gives 22 August, so use "mid-August" if unsure. Existing GPTs kept working.
- OpenAI then announced on 11 September that it plans to retire custom GPTs across plans. Multiple outlets and the OpenAI Developer Community cite OpenAI's help centre for **11 December 2026** as the date they stop running, with Enterprise deferrals to 11 February 2027. OpenAI's own FAQ page blocked my fetch, so **open it yourself and confirm the date before it goes in copy**: https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001519-custom-gpt-retirement-and-migration-faq
- Migration moves a GPT's instructions into a "plugin" as a skill and copies knowledge files across. The selected model, custom actions and drafts do not move. Old conversations stay readable.
- A migrated plugin starts private. Sharing settings do not carry over, sharing outside the workspace is not supported and a redirect from the old link does not grant access to the new plugin.

## Pain themes, strongest first

1. **The link you sold access through goes dark.** Creators sold or shared GPTs by link in memberships, trainings, bundles, client deliverables, newsletters, QR codes and course material. After retirement the old page is offline and customers cannot follow to a replacement. Their words: those URLs are "part of the published work itself".
2. **Customers cannot come with you.** Access to the original does not give access to a replacement. Creators say they have nothing documented to tell customers.
3. **Money and livelihood.** One educator estimated their GPTs brought 50 to 100 paying subscriptions. Another said the change effectively shut their business down. Others describe uncertainty over future revenue.
4. **Time thrown away.** Hundreds of hours of instructions, knowledge files and testing. Some had dozens or hundreds of GPTs. One solopreneur with no IT staff described an unplanned technology project landing in peak season.
5. **Short notice.** One poster said they had "less than 8 hours" before the creation change. Treat as one person's report. The brief says "very little notice", so use that.
6. **Migration does not reproduce the bot.** Actions need a custom server to rebuild. The model choice is lost. Replies reportedly became shorter and prompts needed rewriting. One user reported about 15% of their usage vanished after migrating two GPTs and another said one plugin consumed most of their usage limits.
7. **Trust is gone.** Several say repeated undisclosed changes make the platform hard to rely on. One felt penalised for investing early. Some are reconsidering their premium subscription.
8. **Instructions leak.** The workaround of sharing a conversation shows recipients the instructions. A GPT link had let creators share access without exposing instructions and knowledge files.
9. **Money never arrived.** One creator wrote that the promised monetisation went nowhere for most builders and was tested only with a small group of US builders. Single blog, not independent. Do not state as fact.

## What people say they have tried

- Rebuilding on another provider's assistant tools or cancelling.
- Backing up instructions and files outside ChatGPT.
- Sharing a plain conversation (leaks the instructions).
- An access code inside a private plugin (works but the plugin cannot be distributed).
- Building directly on the API or an apps toolkit.
- Third-party rebuild-and-share services. The posts promoting these look promotional.
- Independent bot platforms and portable "skill file" marketplaces. Almost every guide recommending these comes from a vendor, so treat as marketing and not as evidence of switching.
- Requests to OpenAI that went unanswered: permanent redirects, carrying over public access settings, a sharing cap such as 20 people, an individual builder plan and a later deadline.

## Candidate worst-true-things for Level 3 hooks

Each is traceable to the sources above. Check the date claim first.

- On 11 December every link you ever sold access through goes dark.
- Your paying customers cannot follow you. A replacement starts private and nobody moves across with it.
- You built an audience on a platform that never let you see or contact them. (From the brief: GPTs gave no way to contact your users and no analytics on them.)
- The model and actions you spent weeks tuning do not come with you.
- The only workaround that keeps your customers shows them your instructions.
