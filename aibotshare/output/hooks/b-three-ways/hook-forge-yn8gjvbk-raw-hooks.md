# STEP 1 — EMOTIONAL EXCAVATION (FINAL)

The core emotion is **the specific humiliation of being a digital product seller who can't sell the most important digital product of the decade.** You've made money selling recycled PDFs and course bundles for years — products you know are mediocre — and now you've built something genuinely valuable (an AI bot) and there's literally nowhere on earth to list it with the licensing model you've used your entire career. The identity threat is the worst kind: you're supposed to be the savvy one, the early mover, the person who sees the wave coming. But right now a 22-year-old with no marketing skills is making money with AI while your bot rots on your desktop. The wound that can't be ignored: **an internet marketer GAVE AWAY a bot for free on social media because no platform would let him sell it with resale rights.** That's the emotional center of this entire ad. This is **"the bot resale rights ad"** — the bot, the resale rights, or the marketplace death leads every hook.

## ⚡ TOP 3 MOST DANGEROUS

**⚡ HOOK 1** — "An internet marketer I know spent three months building a custom AI bot that outperforms most paid courses in his niche... He ended up giving it away for free on social media just to get some use out of it." This is the most dangerous hook in the set because it tells a story that every IMer with a bot will recognize as their own nightmare. "Three months" is specific enough to feel real. "Outperforms most paid courses" raises the stakes — this isn't a toy, it's a genuinely superior product. And "gave it away for free" is the emotional kill shot. No marketer can read that line without feeling it in their stomach. The hook never resolves WHY this happened, which forces the click.

**⚡ HOOK 5** — "Somewhere right now, a person with zero marketing skills is making money with AI while you — the person who's been selling digital products for years — have a working bot on your desktop that you literally cannot sell." This is dangerous because it attacks the reader's core identity as a savvy digital marketer. Being outearned by a novice is humiliating enough, but the specific detail of having a WORKING product you CAN'T SELL makes it worse — it's not that you're lazy or behind, it's that the infrastructure betrayed you. The dash construction forces the reader to see themselves in the middle of the sentence.

**⚡ HOOK 6** — "They shut down the biggest GPT marketplace overnight, no warning, no explanation, and thousands of bot builders woke up the next morning with products they could no longer sell." This is dangerous because it names the betrayal directly and uses conspiracy-adjacent language ("they," "no warning, no explanation") that validates the reader's existing anger. "Woke up the next morning" creates a visceral scene — you can feel the moment of checking your phone and realizing it's gone. The second line ("you already know the rage of building on someone else's platform") turns observation into accusation and makes it impossible to scroll past without confronting your own experience.
