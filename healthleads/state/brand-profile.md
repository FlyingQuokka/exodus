## Audience Concerns

- **What's actually IN this, beyond the active ingredient?** The core audience concern this brand exists to answer — fillers, binders, unnecessary additives in mainstream supplements. This is the single biggest lever for this brand specifically, unlike a marketplace brand.
- **Is "additive-free since 1998" just a marketing line, or can they prove it?** Longevity + naming their own manufacturing facility (West Wales) is the brand's answer — specificity beats a vague "trusted since" claim.
- **Is this a small/niche brand I can actually rely on for repeat orders?** Unlike a marketplace giant, trust here has to be built product-by-product and via consistent quality, not sheer scale — Trustpilot sentiment (quality, delivery, value) is the current evidence.
- **For the alt-health/detox segment specifically (wormwood, iodine, parasite-cleanse buyers):** these buyers are further along and more skeptical of mainstream health messaging — they're looking for a brand that takes their specific protocol seriously, not one that treats it as a fringe afterthought next to basic vitamins.

## Brand Voice

Grounded and specific rather than hype-driven — leans on facts (ingredient lists, manufacturing location, founding year) instead of superlatives. Personal and origin-story-driven: the brand exists because of one family's real health experience, and that authenticity is a genuine differentiator worth keeping present in copy, not buried in an About page. Plainspoken, low on exclamation points and urgency language — this is a considered-purchase brand, not an impulse-buy one. Confident about formulation choices without being preachy or fear-mongering about competitors.

## Awareness Primers

### Unaware / Problem-Aware

Original example patterns (greenfield — no existing ad creative to pattern-match, since this brand doesn't run paid ads):

**Pattern: Ingredient-label skepticism hook.** "Flip over your multivitamin. That's not a vitamin — that's a list of fillers with a vitamin somewhere in the middle. We've made ours the other way around since 1998." Works because it turns a passive purchase decision (grabbing whatever's on the shelf) into an active moment of scrutiny, which is exactly this brand's strength.

**Pattern: Founder-crisis origin hook.** "When [founder]'s father got sick, she started reading supplement labels the way a detective reads evidence. What she found changed how she thought supplements should be made." Works because origin stories rooted in real stakes build trust faster than feature lists, especially for a smaller brand without iHerb-scale social proof to lean on instead.

### Solution-Aware / Product-Aware

**Pattern: Manufacturing-transparency close.** "Made in our own facility in West Wales — not a co-packer we've never seen, not a formula we license out. If you've been comparing 'clean' supplement brands, ask them where theirs are actually made." Works on a solution-aware buyer already comparing options — specificity is the differentiator, not a generic quality claim.

**Pattern: Health-goal specific offer.** "Sleep still off? Start where we'd start: [named product], dosed the way we'd take it ourselves." Works on a product-aware buyer who already trusts the brand but needs a nudge toward a specific product rather than the whole catalog — mirrors the site's own "Shop by Health Goals" structure.

### Positioning signals (from About/Our Story page)
