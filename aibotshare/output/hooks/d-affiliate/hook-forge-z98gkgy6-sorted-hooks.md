## Present-Tense Loss / Real-Time Theft

*The reader is losing money RIGHT NOW — the single most detonating pattern in the set.*

**[R] HOOK 6 (Pool B)** — The cruelest part about building a buyer list during the GPT wave isn't that the wave ended. It's that those buyers are STILL spending money every single month on AI tools, and the only thing that changed is they stopped spending it with you. The money never dried up. You just stepped out of the stream and let someone else stand where you were. — **L3**

**[P] HOOK 2 (Pool A)** — Your list already bought. That's the part that should terrify you. Displaced GPT creators don't need to be convinced that they need a new platform. They needed one the day OpenAI shut them down. So they're signing up for whatever lands in their inbox first. If that's not your affiliate link, someone else earns on your subscriber every month for as long as they stay. — **L3**

**[P] HOOK 6 (Pool A)** — Your list is solving the GPT problem without you. While you're reading this, your subscribers are finding new platforms through other people's recommendations, other people's emails, other people's affiliate links. Every one of those signups could have been yours. And unlike a one-time payout, these commissions recur every single month. — **L3**

**[P] HOOK 1 (Pool A)** — Someone on your list signed up for a new bot platform this week and you earned nothing on it. Next week it'll be five more. The week after that, ten. The GPT migration is happening on your list right now and every signup that doesn't go through your link is a recurring commission you will never get back. — **L3**

**[R] HOOK 3 (Pool B)** — The person who bought your GPT six months ago just paid someone else for the third month in a row, and they don't even remember you're the one who introduced them to AI tools in the first place. That's not a cold list. That's a paying customer you raised from scratch and then abandoned at the altar. — **L3**

**[R] HOOK 9 (Pool B)** — A buyer on your list right now, someone who gave you their email and their credit card and their trust, is about to get an email from someone else promoting the exact kind of AI tool you should have found first. They're going to click it. They're going to buy it. And they're going to keep paying for it every month. And you're going to get nothing, not because you lost them, but because you never showed up with the next thing. — **L3**

**[P] HOOK 3 (Pool A)** — "Where do I go now that GPTs are gone?" Your list members are asking each other that question right now in DMs and comment threads, and the answers they're getting are other people's affiliate links. Every one of those signups pays the referring affiliate recurring commissions, month after month. — **L3**

## Confession / First-Person Story Open

*A real-time admission that the reader projects onto themselves.*

**[R] HOOK 2 (Pool B)** — I found out one of my best GPT buyers, a guy who used to reply to my emails by first name, had been paying a competitor I'd never even heard of for six straight months. Not once. Not a one-time buy. Six monthly payments to someone who never spent a single dollar acquiring him. That's when I realized my list wasn't dead... my list was spending money every month and I was the only one not getting paid. — **L3**

**[R] HOOK 5 (Pool B)** — I checked my email stats last Tuesday and found something that made me sick to my stomach: 3,200 people who bought AI bots from me hadn't opened a single email in 90 days. They didn't unsubscribe. That's the part that gutted me. They were still on my list, still paying for AI tools somewhere else, still spending money every month... they just stopped expecting anything from ME because I'd given them nothing worth opening for three months straight. — **L3**

**[R] HOOK 8 (Pool B)** — I spent 11 months building a list of 4,000 AI buyers and then went completely silent for 6 weeks because I had nothing to promote. When I finally checked my stats, 40% of them had stopped opening my emails entirely. Not because they lost interest in AI. Because three other marketers found something worth emailing about before I did, and my buyers became their buyers while I sat there with an empty content calendar. — **L3**

**[R] HOOK 4 (Pool B)** — You did the single hardest thing in online business: you got a stranger to pull out their wallet and trust you with their money. Then you did the single dumbest thing: you let them walk away with nowhere else to buy from you, and now someone who never earned that trust collects a payment from them every single month while you get nothing. — **L3**

**[R] HOOK 1 (Pool B)** — Your buyers forgot your name, and they did it while paying someone else $47 a month for an AI tool you could have sent them yourself. You built that list. You earned that trust. You did the part that takes years. And then you handed those people to a stranger for free because you had nothing else to offer them after your last GPT launch. — **L3**

**[R] HOOK 7 (Pool B)** — Nobody talks about the one-hit-wonder epidemic happening right now in the AI space. Thousands of people built real buyer lists selling GPTs, made real money, felt like real business owners for the first time in their lives... and now they're sitting on those lists with nothing to sell, watching their open rates bleed out week after week while they tell themselves they're "looking for the right offer." — **L3**

They need a link to a platform that lets them do it again. The only question is whose link they click. — **L3**

That's the expensive part. That's the part people burn thousands of dollars in ads trying to create. **[P] HOOK 5 (Pool A)** — You've sent a hundred affiliate emails and watched a hundred commission dashboards go silent after month one. Now your list is full of displaced GPT creators who are actively searching for a new home, and there's a platform paying recurring commissions on every referral for as long as the member stays. This is the launch where "recurring" actually means something. — **L2** *(opens strong on the dead-dashboard memory but resolves into offer language)*

## Mechanism / Feature Tease

*Leads with how the product works rather than emotional loss.*

**[P] HOOK 7 (Pool A)** — Your list already understands PLR. They buy and resell digital rights every day. So when you tell them they can now buy a bot, resell it under their own name, or sell access through a paywall — they don't need it explained. They need a link. AI Bot Share built the Bot Market around the exact rights model your subscribers already use. Three sale types. One published fee. No mystery payout formula. — **L2** *(smart "they already get it" angle, but opens on mechanism, not stakes)*

**[P] HOOK 8 (Pool A)** — Here's an angle nobody's talking about yet: you don't just earn when you refer someone to AI Bot Share. Sellers on the Bot Market can set an affiliate share on each listing, which means other Botters can promote and sell their bots and earn a cut on every sale. You can refer members AND promote their bots. Two commission streams from the same platform. — **L1** *(who-cares)* *(pure feature reveal; no emotional stake, no loss, no identity threat in the opening)*

**[P] HOOK 10 (Pool A)** — What if you could demo the product you're promoting right on your sales page? AI Bot Share lets you embed a working bot on any web page. Put one on your promo page that answers your list's questions about the platform in real time, before they click away. You're not just promoting AI Bot Share. You're proving it works while they watch. — **L1** *(who-cares)* *(clever meta-angle but leads with a soft "what if" and never raises a stake)*

## Strategist's Note

**Types that dominated.** Two patterns carried the set: **present-tense loss / real-time theft** (7 hooks) and **confession / first-person story** (3 hooks). Together they account for the overwhelming majority of the L3s. The brand's whole emotional thesis — "it's happening right now, without you" — lives in these two families.

**How the pools differed.** The primed pool (A) is laser-locked on the *mechanism of loss*: the recurring commission, the migration happening on the list, the competitor's link. It's surgical and consistent, but it dips into feature-tease territory in its back third (Hooks 7, 8, 10) — those are the only L1s in the entire document, and all three are primed. The raw pool (B) never once wandered into feature language. Instead it reached for **concrete, embodied detail** — "$47 a month," "replied by first name," "3,200 people," "11 months, 6 weeks, 40%," "abandoned at the altar," "stepped out of the stream." Pool B's confessions (2, 5, 8) are a texture the primed pool never produced, and they're some of the most detonating hooks here. Net: **A is more strategically disciplined; B is more viscerally alive.** The ideal winner borrows A's precision about *recurring commission* and B's sensory specifics.

**L3 count.** 15 of 20 hooks landed L3. 3 landed L2 (Pool A #5, #7, #10-Pool-B). 2 landed L1 / who-cares (Pool A #8, #10). Every who-cares flag went to a primed, feature-led hook.

**Three I'd test first:**
1. **[R] Pool B HOOK 2** — the "replied by first name / competitor I'd never heard of / six straight months" confession. Most specific, most projectable, highest flinch.
2. **[R] Pool B HOOK 6** — the single best *reframe* in the set ("the money never dried up, you just stepped out of the stream"); it demolishes the reader's last excuse.
3. **[P] Pool A HOOK 2** — "Your list already bought. That's the part that should terrify you." — the tightest primed hook, five words to full emotional payload, and the cleanest expression of the recurring-commission stake.

Test note: run one B-confession against one A-mechanism-loss head-to-head early — it's the fastest way to learn whether this audience converts harder on *embodied story* or on *precise financial framing*.
