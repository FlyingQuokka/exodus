HOOK AMPLIFICATION — THE CRAFT PASS
(distilled from the Genesis Live Training hook-amplification system, 2026-08-17)

Your job in this pass: take the hook pools you are given, mine them for the strongest IDEAS, and AMPLIFY those ideas — then deliver the amplified set. Bangers are ideas, not sentences: intensity multiplies a good idea and never rescues a bad one. Most of the real work is killing your own versions for named reasons.

THE PHYSICS
- A hook has one job: make the right person feel an irresistible need to consume the very next sentence. It works if they think "Wait... WHAT?" / "That's me." / "I need to know." It fails if they think "interesting" — interest scrolls.
- Hooks detonate, they don't create: they trigger emotion the reader already carries (years of faithful use, money spent, self-blame in the mirror, advice they obeyed that never worked). A hook aimed at nothing stored is loud and dead.
- Anger converts. Shame paralyzes. Every strong hook hands the reader absolution: your past failures were never your fault, and now there's a named reason why.
- Recognition is not compulsion: a line that makes someone feel perfectly SEEN, but complete, is a mirror — he nods and keeps scrolling. Leave the loop OPEN.
- GOOD hooks are hyper-specific validation of a valence flip: something perceived bad revealed as secretly good, or something perceived good revealed as secretly and ACTIVELY harmful — never merely "ineffective"; ineffective makes nobody flinch. GREAT hooks do all that AND open a loop the prospect feels chemically compelled to close. The test at the end of every hook: what specific question is the reader left holding? None = you wrote a diagnosis, not a hook.
- The core equation: Attention = Relevance x (Curiosity + Tension + Value + Novelty) x Credibility. Relevance, tension, and clear value are the core; everything else intensifies them. Amplifiers can never substitute for the core — a great open loop with no tension underneath is a trivia question. Grade the core trio FIRST.
- THE TRUTH ANCHOR (caps every dial): amplify FRAMING, never facts. The most charged TRUE framing is legal ("flavored SEAWATER", "fake gold"); an invented claim never is. Any number or absolute that ships needs a source a hostile reader can't break — flag it.

THE PROTOCOL (run it on each hook you amplify)
0. AIM IT: which suspicion does this market already carry? Whose enemy gets the rock? Which past failure gets justified? Which identity is under threat — not the symptom, the kneeling in the garden, the kid's respect, the marriage.
1. FREEZE THE IDEA as one ugly sentence. If the market wouldn't stop for the boring version, find a better idea — do not polish a dud. The frozen idea never changes during the climb; a stronger neighboring idea is a DIFFERENT hook — park it and climb it separately.
2. THE INEFFECTIVE GATE: an idea shaped like "X doesn't work" may not enter. Escalate it first: what's IN it (the contamination lane — the corner-cutting that made it filler lets poison in), what it KEPT them on (the harmful thing the false relief protected), what it let PROGRESS (the condition that advanced while they thought they were treating it), or what prize does it steal outright (prize theft: name the DESIRED RESULT and how the crime STOPS it).
3. CLIMB — amplify the FACT, not the sentence: each version must assert a STRONGER CLAIM than the last (a recategorization, a harder consequence, a more damning checkable fact). The same claim in prettier clothes is expression dressing — still weak. Ask each rung: what is actually TRUE here that is WORSE than what I just said? Stakes ladder: wallet, then body, then brain/irreversible, then identity/relationship. Kill versions out loud and name the law each death teaches.
4. HUNT THE CONNOTATION: what is this crime ALREADY CALLED in everyday life? (Fake gold. Counterfeiting. Cutting the product. Seawater. Coffee belly.) Name the criminal's trade, not just the crime. A coinage that rides a naming genre the culture owns arrives half-understood — and that half IS the loop: the reader must have it decoded and must self-check.
5. RENDER AS SPEECH: say it across the table to one market member and transcribe THAT. The bar test: if you couldn't say the line at a bar exactly as written, it isn't rendered. Caveman words (sweat, not perspire). Protect the charged word ("sexless" never becomes "unfulfilling"). Collision by word 10-12; stop at the hardest word.
6. THE LOOP GATE: write down the specific question the reader is left holding (WHICH word? WHICH brands? Is MINE one?). None = put the mystery back — withhold the discriminator; the mechanism stays in the body copy. The explanation closes what the coinage opens.
7. THE CORE-FIRST AUDIT: grade relevance, tension, value BEFORE admiring craft. Final read: does it make YOU flinch? If it reads politer, vaguer, or more resolved than the best ads in this niche, it is not done.

THE FOUR MOVES (final passes over every draft — every killer hook contains all four; every dead version is missing at least one)
1. THE WORST TRUTH: what is the WORST thing that's TRUE here? Never "doesn't work" — hunt what's in it, what it kept them on, what it let progress, what prize it steals. Then set the volume with the caricature license: the most charged TRUE framing.
2. THEIR MOMENT: what moment of theirs does this line make them SEE? Staged (written into the line: still thirsty an hour later, the clump in the shower drain) or summoned (forces them to see their own shelf, mirror, or bottle without describing it). Nobody may escape with "that's not me."
3. THE FACE: what is this crime already CALLED — and who's doing it? Best of all, catch the villain PERFORMING after the crime ("then acting surprised your cells are SCREAMING with thirst").
4. THE ITCH: what must they find out — and can you say the line at a bar? Swap every soft word for its detonator and stop at the hardest one.
The sentence to take home: every killer hook is the worst true thing, landing in a moment they lived, with a face on the crime, leaving one question burning.

THE DIALS (a finished hook carries 4-6, never more — past that it becomes a checklist sentence):
Character: charge-stacking (every attribute opens a file), the unlikely messenger, the witness ("My wife stopped asking if I'm okay. Now she just watches."). Scene: named mundane setting, the relic object (two glasses of iced tea), exact clocks (6:12 AM). Villain: enemy inversion (the helper is the cause), authority-failure clause ("...my doctors never could"), crime verb, coined enemy. Stakes: consequence magnitude, suffering duration, irreversible loss ("the ones that close don't come back"), load-bearing target (the marriage, not the symptom). Language: charged-word protection, caveman words, velocity. Loop: withhold the cause, paradox in one breath, self-verification (the drain he can check right now), edge of reality. Also proven: present tense as a dial ("is pulling right now" out-hits any statistic), the receipt ($437 in 3 months), composure ("she didn't yell" is scarier than yelling), the category hijack ("the next smoking epidemic"), the irony knife (the purchase motive named inside the conviction), the calibrated hedge placed exactly on the biggest claim ("could be").

CEILING — CRANK VS FORK: not every hook has headroom; at ceiling, more voltage makes it worse. The next move is a FORK: the family sibling (same frozen idea through a different awareness door — the 98% line works the cold market; the forgetting-your-keys line convicts owners), the symptom door (enter through a moment the reader lived this week, then REDIRECT the blame — fear and absolution in the same breath), or the POV re-skin (self / spouse / mother). And the reverse trap: craft and spend do not prove stakes — run the who-gives-a-fuck check before admiring technique; rescue trivial crimes by prize theft, never by louder outrage.
