## Audience Concerns

- **Will this actually be good quality, not just cheap?** The brand's biggest praise is price/selection, so buyers need reassurance that low prices don't mean low quality — brand partnerships (Optimum Nutrition, L'Oréal, Nature's Bounty) and product transparency (visible expiration dates) are the proof points that work here, not vague "trusted" claims.
- **Where do I even start with 20,000 products?** Overwhelm is a real barrier for a first-time or casual wellness shopper — the doctor-led "3 supplements to start with" format exists specifically because this concern is common.
- **What happens if something goes wrong with my order?** Public sentiment shows a real gap here (slow support response, shipping/packaging damage). Don't oversell customer service as a strength — lead with product/price/selection instead, and if addressing this concern at all, be concrete (e.g. specific refund/replacement policy) rather than reassuring in the abstract.
- **Is this brand legit / will my payment info be safe on a marketplace I haven't used before?** Standard for any large online retailer — social proof at scale (12.5M+ customers, 29 years) is the brand's existing answer to this.

## Brand Voice

Approachable and benefit-forward, not clinical or jargon-heavy. Leans on concrete numbers over vague claims ("12.5M+ customers," "29 years," "$98M+ credits rewarded") to build trust fast. Uses light, occasional emoji for visual pop (✨🌿🛒) without overloading copy. Frames wellness as part of everyday life ("morning runs," "weekend games") rather than a specialist/clinical pursuit. Direct, short sentences; conversational questions as hooks ("Not sure where to start with your wellness routine?"). Confident about value/price without sounding discount-desperate.

## Awareness Primers

### Unaware / Problem-Aware

Winning-pattern examples (originals written to match observed high-performing angles — not verbatim iHerb copy):

**Pattern: Credentialed-expert explainer.** A doctor or nutrition expert on camera, addressing overwhelm directly: "Don't know where to start with your wellness routine? A doctor breaks down the 3 supplements worth starting with — no guesswork, no 47-tab research rabbit hole." Works because it turns a paralysis-inducing amount of choice into a simple, trusted shortlist.

**Pattern: Everyday-moment lifestyle hook.** Non-clinical framing tying a product benefit to a relatable daily moment: "That 3pm slump isn't just 'you.' Real energy for real moments — from the morning run to the school pickup — starts with what you're actually giving your body." Works because it reframes a vague, unnamed problem (fatigue) into something specific and solvable without sounding medical.

### Solution-Aware / Product-Aware

**Pattern: Big-number social proof.** "Join 12.5M+ people who've already made [category] part of their routine — real results, backed by [X] years of experience, not just marketing claims." Works on a solution-aware audience because they've already decided they want a solution; the job here is de-risking the specific choice, and scale/tenure is the fastest way to do that.

**Pattern: Value-anchored retargeting offer.** "Still deciding? Here's what's actually in your cart's price range: [named product], [discount/rewards detail], free delivery over [threshold]." Works on product-aware shoppers who know the brand already — the barrier left is usually price hesitation, not trust, so the ad should answer that directly instead of re-explaining who the brand is.
