## Strategist's Note

**Correction on duplication:** I flagged one listing error above. The hook "You can sell master resell rights to a 12-page PDF…" belongs to **Pool B (raw)** only. It appears once in the source and is listed once correctly as [R]; the extra line with the stray [P] tag at the top of the first group is an artifact and should be disregarded — only the [R] version is real. No source hook was dropped; total unique hooks = 20 (10 primed, 10 raw).

**Which types dominated.** The two pools split cleanly by instinct. The **primed pool** gravitated toward *Identity Demolition* and *Present-Tense Accusation* — hooks that weaponize the reader's own expertise against them ("you teach this for a living," "called it a business," "the landlord changed but the lease didn't"). The **raw pool** gravitated toward *Confession/Story*, *Betrayal/Rug-Pull*, and *News/Category Opening* — hooks that point at an external event or a third party rather than the reader's own shame.

**How primed differed from raw.** Primed hooks land the emotional bomb inside the first 1–2 lines and make the *reader* the guilty party ("Your $17 PLR ebook has three revenue paths"). Raw hooks more often open on someone else ("a marketer I know," "they shut down the marketplace") and take a beat longer to implicate the reader. Raw also leaned harder on "nobody has noticed / the window is closing" FOMO framing, which is where most of the **(who-cares)** flags clustered — those hooks describe a market trend rather than a personal wound, so a reader can nod and scroll.

**How many hit L3.** By my count **11 hooks reach L3** — 7 primed, 4 raw. The primed pool's L3 rate is the real story: nearly every primed hook detonates immediately because the primer forced the $17 contrast and the "cobbler's children" identity threat into the opening words. The raw pool's best work (the "gave it away for free" story, the rug-pull, the "zero skills" comparison) is genuinely strong, but half the raw set stays at L2 trend-commentary.

**The 3 I'd test first:**
1. **[P]** "You accepted mystery money on your most profitable asset for months and called it a business…" — four-word identity kill ("called it a business") plus an immediate concrete escape (published fee).
2. **[P]** "Your $17 PLR ebook has three revenue paths…" — fastest detonation in the set; the dollar figure does the damage in eight words.
3. **[R]** "An internet marketer I know spent three months building a custom AI bot… He ended up giving it away for free…" — best of the raw pool and the only story-open that works as a pure open loop; test it as the narrative counterweight to the two accusatory primed hooks.
